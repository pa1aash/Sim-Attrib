\begin{abstract}
Attributing a simulator's misspecification to one component presupposes that the available
summaries can tell the components apart -- a precondition that can fail quietly. We check it,
on a three-component epidemic simulator under an adversarial threat model, using
rank-and-condition-number diagnostics. One summary set separates all three components under
every one of eight distortion assignments tried, given at most one distortion parameter per
component; the same measurement locates exactly where this breaks once a component carries two
parameters at once ($\kappa=628.9$, rank 4 of 6), on a real, interpretable confound. A
selective-inference construction for turning this diagnostic into a calibrated attribution test
is affordable at a known parameter but fails to terminate once the nuisance parameters are only
known to the precision a maximum-likelihood fit to the data gives them -- a box wider, on every
coordinate, than one already shown to break it. The boundary is predicted before it is
measured, from the ratio of nuisance shift to observation noise, and the prediction holds.
Every tool used is prior art; the contribution is the finding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A simulator used for scientific inference is essentially always misspecified somewhere.
Existing SBI tests ask whether it is misspecified at all; a practitioner who gets a positive
answer usually wants to know \emph{which part} to fix, not only that something needs fixing.
Attributing misspecification to a specific component presupposes something the detection
question does not need: that the available summary statistics can actually tell the components
apart. That precondition can fail quietly, and a procedure that reports a confident attribution
without checking it first is reporting a number the data may not support.

This paper checks that precondition on a canonical case -- a three-component compartmental
epidemic simulator, with transmission,
progression, and observation as the components -- and reports four findings, none a claim
about a new method (Section~\ref{sec:background}):

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:background}

\begin{itemize}[topsep=1pt,itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Detecting misspecification versus attributing it.} SBI has moved from asking
whether a simulator is misspecified to asking where -- \citet{Cranmer_2020} name summary
informativeness and misspecification diagnosis among the field's open problems.
\citet{schmitt2022detectingmodelmisspecificationamortized} flag out-of-distribution embeddings
with one discrepancy score; \citet{ward2022robustneuralposteriorestimation} model the
summary-level error explicitly; \citet{Frazier_2020} give a shrinking-tolerance ABC diagnostic
for posteriors concentrating under misspecification. Each is a global or per-summary signal
that something is wrong, not which component caused it -- the question this paper asks instead.
\citet{montel2025testsmodelmisspecificationsimulationbased}'s family of tests, our baseline,
corrects for multiplicity via a trials-corrected $p$-value on the \emph{minimum} of per-summary
statistics -- the global null that something is misspecified, not the identity of the
arg\,min. On our six-column confound (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}) their global test would
correctly reject, but its arg\,min is exactly our equivalence-class ambiguity, which their
local tests would flag on both sides rather than resolve.
\item \textbf{The identifiability precondition.} \citet{Kahl_2019} answer it exactly in a
closely related setting: per-component input reconstruction in a dynamic system is identifiable
iff a structural sensitivity map has full column rank, resting on \citet{Sain_1969}'s
invertibility condition. \citet{CatchpoleMorgan_1997} give the general parameter-redundancy
framework this instantiates; \citet{Brynjarsd_ttir_2014} frame the broader model-discrepancy
problem for computer-model calibration; \citet{Miao_2011} give the differential-algebra route
on epidemic-ODE models directly -- two parameters entering only through a fixed combination are
non-identifiable regardless of design, exactly our six-column confound's failure mode -- and
\citet{Raue_2009}'s profile likelihood is the numerical diagnostic for the same failure under
noisy data. We treat all six as an established precondition, not a result of this paper.
\item \textbf{Diagnostic tooling.} Screening a sensitivity matrix's rank and condition number
to decide whether parameters are jointly recoverable is not new; \citet{Cintr_n_Arias_2009}
apply exactly this screen to a compartmental epidemic model, the class we use in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Our Jacobian is simulation-estimated rather than symbolic, so we
also need to know how far a finite-difference estimate can be trusted under simulation noise;
\citet{Mor__2011} and \citet{Mor__2012} give the relevant results, of which our step-size sweep
(Section~\ref{sec:method}) is a coarser, pre-registered substitute. Whether the spectrum has a
clean gap or decays smoothly -- where any rank tolerance is partly a statement about where the
analyst put it -- is \citet{Gutenkunst_2007}'s general objection; Section~\ref{sec:experiments}
measures rather than assumes an answer.
\item \textbf{Exact inference under a composite, simulation-only null.}
Section~\ref{sec:negative-result} evaluates a natural construction for turning the diagnostic's
output into a calibrated test: exact finite-sample inference under a null depending on unknown
nuisance parameters, by maximizing a simulated $p$-value over the nuisance set, is
\citet{Dufour_2006}'s maximized Monte Carlo test; calibrating a selection event with no
analytic description, by rejection-sampling the simulator's own null until a replicate lands in
the observed cell, is available for arbitrary conditioning events following
\citet{freidling2026selectiverandomizationinferenceadaptive}. Composing the two lets a
simulator supply everything both need without further modelling assumptions. Dufour's is not
the only route through a composite null: \citet{xie2022reprosamplesmethodfinite}'s repro
samples and \citet{Barber_2022}'s approximate co-sufficient sampling both discharge it by
conditioning out or maximizing over the nuisance direction, and both would, like Dufour's,
absorb our two confounded distortion parameters together rather than attribute a rejection to
either individually.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Scope assumption, stated first.}
Throughout this paper we assume each simulator component is misspecified by at most one
mechanism at a time: every component carries a single one-parameter distortion family, never
two at once. This is load-bearing rather than a footnote (Section~\ref{sec:experiments} shows
what happens once it is dropped), so we state it before the diagnostic's machinery.

\paragraph{The diagnostic.}
In four steps (every ingredient prior art, Section~\ref{sec:background}):
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=1pt,itemsep=1pt,parsep=0pt,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Declare.} The simulator as $K$ components, each carrying a one-parameter distortion
family $\delta_k(\cdot\,;\eta_k)$ bit-identical to the base simulator at $\eta_k=0$, asserted
bit-for-bit before anything else runs.
\item \textbf{Simulate.} Estimate the summary Jacobian $J=\partial s/\partial\eta$ at $\eta=0$ by
central differences under common random numbers, swept over six decades of step size $h$;
without common random numbers the difference is dominated by noise of order $\sigma/h$ and no
stable range exists (confirmed by a negative control).
\item \textbf{Normalise and take the SVD.} Summaries are divided by their prior-predictive
standard deviation and each $\eta_k$ by a fixed relative perturbation scale -- both, and every
tolerance below, fixed before any singular value existed. The diagnostic's only input is the
summary vector; it never receives a component identity or a ground-truth label, so there is no
hidden truth available to leak into its answer.
\item \textbf{Decide.} Components are \emph{separable} if $J$'s condition number satisfies
$\kappa \le \kappa_{\max}=1/\tau$ -- equivalently, full column rank at tolerance $\tau$, since
$\kappa \le 1/\tau$ restates rank deficiency at $\tau$ rather than adding a second requirement;
we report both because each is the more natural unit in different places below. We use
$\tau=10^{-2}$, $\kappa_{\max}=100$, from a compute budget: resolving components to precision
$\kappa$ costs order $\kappa^2$ replicates (a singular value as small as $\sigma_1/\kappa$
needs the per-entry standard error below it, and that error falls as
$(\text{replicates})^{-1/2}$); $\kappa=100$ exhausts this study's budget. A singular value
varying by more than a factor of two across the plateau is \emph{unresolved} and counted toward
the rank in neither direction, a third outcome distinct from separable/not, so the reported rank
can be an interval rather than a single number. Resolution is a property of the
\emph{estimator}; whether the underlying spectrum has a clean gap or decays smoothly across many
decades is a property of the \emph{matrix}, and passing the resolution test answers the first
question, not the second (Section~\ref{sec:background}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Where separability fails.}
When the rank condition fails, we report the \emph{equivalence class} named by each near-null
right singular vector $v$ ($\sigma \le \tau\sigma_1$) rather than a single culprit: component
$k$ joins if $|v_k|\ge 0.3$, comfortably below both the two-component ($\approx 0.707$) and
three-component ($\approx 0.577$) confound values. No arrow leaves the verdict: the diagnostic
decides whether attribution is well posed, and nothing further.

\paragraph{Simulator and summary sets.}
We apply the diagnostic to the $K=3$ compartmental epidemic simulator of
Figure~\ref{fig:simulator}. We evaluate three summary sets: $S_A$, epidemic-curve features a
domain modeller would choose (peak height, peak time, final size, growth rate); $S_B$, roughly
ten time-binned incidence counts an SBI practitioner would choose; and $S_C$, an impoverished
positive control with two summaries, which \emph{must} fail by construction ($d=2<K=3$) and
does -- rank 2, $\kappa=\infty$. A random-attributor floor of $1/K$ (analytic $0.3333$,
measured $0.3299$) is the baseline any component-attribution accuracy is read against.

\paragraph{Where attribution is identifiable: $S_B$ under eight family assignments.}
Combining the two declared families component-wise gives eight three-column distortion models
(Figure~\ref{fig:assignments}), only two of which -- all-base and all-adversarial -- were
originally declared; the other six recombine the same columns at no additional simulation cost.
Under our scope assumption, $S_B$ separates all three components under \emph{all eight}:
condition number $6.628$ to $65.64$, every singular value resolved, no coherence pair flagged.
$S_A$ is a control, not a result: it splits exactly on the transmission family, failing at
$\kappa=100.9$ -- $0.9\%$ past the ceiling -- on the assignment that swaps in only the
adversarial transmission distortion, proof the instrument is not vacuously ``separable.'' The
margin is not uniform: $S_B$'s verdict survives $\tau$ moving by
$9.881\times$ under the base assignment but only $1.523\times$ under the tightest one
(Table~\ref{tab:sb-eight}, Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}), so doubling $\tau$ -- this project's
own coarse robustness check -- flips it. Nor is it an artifact of the flat $10\%$ ETA\_SCALE
unit: rescaling by each parameter's confidence-set standard error instead
(Section~\ref{sec:negative-result}: $0.9$--$2.7\%$, not a common $10\%$) leaves both separable
($\kappa=31.9$, $8.0$; rank $3$ of $3$).

\paragraph{Where it is not: two distortion parameters per component.}
Placing all six declared distortion directions side by side -- two per component -- gives a
single six-column object. For $S_B$ it is \textbf{inseparable} at $\kappa=628.9$, rank $4$ of
$6$ (Figure~\ref{fig:spectrum}): not an unresolved estimator (worst singular-value variation
across the plateau, $1.023$ against an admissible $2$); not a threshold artifact (inseparable
across $\tau$ from $0.005$ to $1.0$, with no tolerance at which the object is both separable
and affordable); and not structural ($d=10\ge6$). Both near-null directions are
cross-mechanism, naming progression together with observation, with transmission carrying
under $5\%$ of the energy in either (Figure~\ref{fig:confound}, Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}):
a drifting removal hazard is nearly indistinguishable from a constant hazard change combined
with a drifting reporting rate. The confound needs progression to carry two
distortion parameters at once -- why it cannot arise under our one-parameter-per-component
scope, and why that restriction, not a bound on $\kappa$, is the assumption's real content.
Under the data-motivated scaling above, $\kappa$ falls to $344.9$ but stays two orders past
$\kappa_{\max}=100$ (rank $5$ of $6$, not $4$: one near-null direction now resolves). In the
six-column case the spectrum matches the gapless, no-clean-break picture
\citet{Gutenkunst_2007}'s sloppy-models literature describes (spread $2.80$ decades, gap
prominence $1.40$, $\tau\sigma_1$ falling inside the spectrum); in the three-column case it does
not. The reassurance a three-column threshold offers is a fact about having three columns, not
about the simulator. ($K$ denotes the three components; column count is separate, written in
words to avoid conflating the two.)

\section{Where exact conditional attribution becomes unaffordable, and why}
\label{sec:negative-result}

Composing Dufour's maximized Monte Carlo with rejection-sampling calibration
(Section~\ref{sec:background}) turns the diagnostic's identifiable case into a candidate for
exact conditional inference on which component gets attributed: comfortably affordable at a
known parameter, but unable to terminate once the nuisance parameters are only known within a
realistic neighborhood. The boundary of that failure is predicted, before it is measured, from
the ratio of nuisance-induced shift to observation noise, and the prediction holds -- and the
neighborhood at which it takes over is not an assumption we chose: it is narrower than the
uncertainty a maximum-likelihood fit to the simulator's own data reports, on every one of the
five nuisance coordinates. The composition controls the selective type-I error conservatively
rather than exactly (both the supremum over the nuisance space and the discrete Monte Carlo
p-value are sources of conservativeness), and its only theorem requires the selection rule to
be a fixed, deterministic function of the data with every admissible nuisance value keeping
the observed selection event reachable. Every tool composed here is established exact-inference
machinery (Section~\ref{sec:background}); we measure the composition against our own simulator.

\paragraph{Affordable at a known parameter, unbounded over a data-implied nuisance set.}
At the true parameter the composition is comfortably affordable: the worst selection cell
holds $23\%$ of null draws, and one test costs $4.2\times10^5$ to $4.3\times10^7$ simulator
draws against a pre-registered gate of $10^8$ -- a clear pass. The question is whether that
affordability survives once the nuisance parameters (transmission and recovery rates, reporting
fraction, initial infections, observation noise scale) are only known to the precision real
data give them, not an arbitrary assumed tolerance. We answer this directly rather than assume
a box: fitting the simulator to one realized dataset by maximum likelihood and building a
Bonferroni-corrected $95\%$ confidence box from the Fisher information gives relative
half-widths from $2.3\%$ to $16.6\%$ -- every one wider than the $\pm0.5\%$ box already known
to break the composition (below). Re-measuring the gate inside this data-implied box, the
acceptance probability collapses to $10^{-5}$ at the primary case and to exactly zero at the
other three declared combinations, and the gate \textbf{fails at every corner under all four}:
the cheapest declared corner alone needs $9.9\times10^9$ draws, two orders of magnitude over
budget before the confidence interval is even taken into account. The collapse below was
measured under a round, assumed $\pm5\%$ box; here it survives, by a wide margin, under the box
the data themselves
imply.

\paragraph{The mechanism, and the shape of the boundary.}
The selection rule's fixed-function requirement means it cannot track $\theta$: the nuisance
parameters shift the normalized summary vector while a single draw's noise magnitude stays
fixed, and once that shift exceeds the noise, a $\theta$-free rule selects one component
deterministically and the cell the data selected becomes unreachable. A sweep over ten relative
half-widths from $0.1\%$ to $5\%$
(Figure~\ref{fig:nontermination}) locates the shape of this collapse under the earlier,
round-number box: gradual rather than abrupt (about one decade of acceptance probability lost
per $0.7\%$ of relative nuisance error, log-linear at $R^2=0.94$), passing at every declared
corner only inside a $\pm0.5\%$ box -- tighter than the data-implied box above supports. The
ratio itself is a portable one-line check for other simulators.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}[topsep=1pt,itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The scope assumption is not a formality.} Every positive result assumes
(Section~\ref{sec:method}) at most one distortion parameter per component -- a real restriction:
the moment one component carries two, the same summary set that separated all three becomes
inseparable on a real confound. We have not tested two distortions of the \emph{same} component.
\item \textbf{The diagnostic is prior art and a local linearization.} The rank-and-condition-number
screen (Section~\ref{sec:method}) leaves an estimated simulation-noise level uncarried into the
rank tolerance; we derive our tolerance from a compute budget instead. $J$ is also a
linearization at $\eta=0$ and at one parameter point: separability of the $10\%$ perturbation our
scale represents is not directly implied by local linear separability. We checked one additional
$\theta$, drawn $\pm20\%$ relative per coordinate before the check ran: $S_B$ stays separable
there too ($\kappa=10.9$, full rank), matching $\theta_0$'s own verdict under the same harness
($\kappa=10.1$) -- one point, not a distribution over $\theta$.
\item \textbf{One simulator, one scope, no learned component.} Every result is on one simulator
under two family sets we chose; the eight assignments are every combination those permit, not a
general sample, and transport to other simulator classes or a learned summary statistic (which
would sit where $s(y)$ sits here) is untested. Two figures also carry hand-placed
annotations outside the automated check -- verified restatements, not independent claims.
\end{itemize}
